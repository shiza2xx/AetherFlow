# Reference and composition

At the composition stage, inspect the actual registered source and current job state and propose the catalog for human review. Refer to generated color images, candidates, or accepted outputs only if the tools confirm they exist, and state their actual status. Planning future views is not evidence that those views were generated. Do not generate image-stage outputs merely because a composition prompt describes the later workflow.

## Establish the target

Read the original full-resolution color artwork and the user's explicit target. A reference may contain many unrelated objects: include only the requested target and its accepted constituent props. Keep the original artifact and optional crop rectangle. A crop must not destroy access to context needed for materials, geometry, or scale.

Collect name, composite-object versus prop-set mode, target, inclusions, exclusions, optional known dimension, and optional crop. Do not demand measurements the reference cannot supply. Record hidden sides, thickness, and inferred dimensions as assumptions for review.

## Building layout from the reference

For a composite building, inspect the registered original artwork, selected crop, and user brief before proposing placements. Count visible habitable storeys separately from attic, dormers, gables, and roof tiers. Note floor bands/elevations, repeated facade and window rows, ridge and eaves, widths, proportions, and major silhouettes. Let the source and brief govern: do not assume a fixed storey count, mistake attic space for a full storey, or hide genuine ambiguity.

Plan a complete architecture-appropriate envelope using suitable wall, roof, beam, window, and door masters in the reviewed catalog and their instances; reuse accepted masters instead of duplicating them, and propose missing masters for human review. Include front, left, right, rear, roof ends/gables, plus the closures and contacts the construction needs. Do not omit an exterior side because it is unseen. Infer hidden architecture consistently from visible style, footprint, materials, and repeated construction; record those inferences in assumptions and mark each affected placement `placementConfidence: inferred` with concise `placementEvidence`. Never present hidden sides as fully source-supported or 100% known. Reuse common wall material and a plausible rear treatment; add windows only when supported by visible evidence or an explicit inference. Do not copy unique front doors or facade details to the rear without evidence. An explicit user exclusion or an open-sided/cutaway design takes precedence.

For multiple storeys, repeat the accepted single-storey masters as instances using actual model bounds and local axes with explicit transforms. Keep floor bands, openings, and roof height aligned with the source proportions; do not stretch one storey to fake several. When correcting accepted placements, change transforms or add instances only. Preserve accepted images, meshes, materials, item IDs, existing instance IDs, slots, and history; give only new instances unique IDs. Keep doors and windows separate from continuous uncut walls; do not cut openings or create new assets without a request. This rule applies to composite buildings, not prop-sets, trees/foliage, or independent props.

## Catalog masters and instances

Propose useful independent assets, not every brick or bolt. Each master has a stable ID, a descriptive user-facing name, a route, required views, source references, and a clear scope. The job-wide optional depth choice applies across the catalog and is off by default for a new job. Repeated identical objects are instances of a master. A substantially different opening, silhouette, or design is a variant, not an arbitrarily stretched instance.

For any building suited to modular construction, examine the reference and propose reusable components instead of making the entire shell a single master. When wall material and construction match, use the same continuous, uncut wall master for areas with and without windows or doors; do not create a separate wall image or model merely to add an opening. Generate windows and doors as independent masters/objects and record their intended positions and scale. The user will remove the relevant wall geometry and install them manually; do not claim an opening was cut or a door was rigged or animated. Different wall materials, textures or genuinely different construction may require distinct continuous wall masters, but an opening alone is not a reason. Treat visible wooden beams and similar structural framing as independently generated, reusable objects, not as geometry or a painted substitute baked into a wall; record their placement and attachment. Roof sections and other structural pieces are also separate where appropriate. Preserve the source design across the assembled parts. Different buildings may require different modules; do not blindly force the house example on indivisible objects, and explain uncertainty in the review proposal. Repeated identical beams, walls, windows, doors or lamps use the same master and materials, with explicit instances and transforms. Keep existing approved records, references and history intact; propose any changes to the user for review rather than silently migrating them.

For each wall master, wall variant and standalone window master, explicitly include both `front` and `side` in that item's catalog views. Do the same for a tiled or pitched roof when the source shows its slope. They are separate images; `side` is a strict 90-degree orthographic profile and communicates thickness, roof pitch and depth. Check the accepted catalog's actual views before generation; do not rely on an optional/default front-only view list. If an existing item lacks its side view, propose adding it to that existing master for human review without discarding its front, instances, history, or unrelated work. With AI depth enabled, each requested color view needs its matching depth role. Other solid objects need a side view only when their form requires it; foliage retains its own four-view rules.

An accepted composite needs at least one active instance to pass stage-5 validation. An omitted `instances` field preserves the current placements; it does not create placements when the current list is empty. A catalog may be accepted before placements are ready, but an empty active list blocks validation, scene building, and export. Prop-set catalogs may omit instances because their items export independently. For repeated parts, reuse the same `masterItemId` but assign a unique instance ID and a distinct evidence-based transform to every occurrence. Never substitute one shared identity transform, coincident parts, or unreviewed assumptions just to satisfy validation. Include `placementConfidence` (`source-supported`, `inferred`, or `uncertain`) and a concise `placementEvidence` note for repaired or inferred placements.

To recover an already accepted composite whose active instance list is empty, preserve the accepted item IDs, source references, geometry/material candidates, artifacts, shared-sheet provenance, slots, and candidate history. Read the original registered source and inspect each current accepted geometry model, including its bounds and local axes, then propose the existing catalog unchanged with explicit placements and assumptions. Submit only a catalog proposal and wait for human review; do not regenerate accepted outputs or accept the proposal through MCP. A single perspective reference cannot establish exact hidden depth or dimensions. Label inferred or uncertain transforms and ask a focused question when an important placement cannot be estimated responsibly. After a person accepts the proposal, rerun validation, build a new scene candidate, inspect it, and request human scene acceptance.

Use routes appropriate to the object:

- Volumetric modules: wall, roof, trunk, large structural part.
- Shared prop-sheet members: small independent props generated together, then segmented.
- Foliage clumps: transparent images and card clusters, never ordinary solid foliage blobs.

The catalog must support arbitrary agent-chosen names and any useful number of props. Do not restrict it to a fixed list of accessory categories. Group inseparable intentional accessories as one asset where appropriate.

## Review and blocking

Present the complete catalog and assumptions in one review, including master/instance relationships and shared sheet membership. A simple proxy/blocking may show planned placement, but label it as a proxy. Final production must replace it with real returned meshes.

Use one shared coordinate convention and units. Keep local transforms distinct from geometry edits. Changing placement must not invalidate image generation or require a replacement model. Avoid nonuniform scaling that visibly deforms trim or materials.

Advance from composition after catalog review, not after merely writing a proposal. Preserve accepted unrelated items during a catalog revision. A new item requires only its own dependent outputs.
