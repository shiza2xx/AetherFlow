# Two-target image contract

Head includes face, eyes, brows, ears, all front/side/back hair and head ornaments. Exclude neck/shoulders/collar/hood. Do not cut long hair or move it to body. Body includes neck, worn outfit/collar/hood, arms/hands, legs/shoes. Do not duplicate ornaments.

Head upright, neutral: level eyes, vertical centerline, relaxed brows, open eyes, naturally closed mouth. Preserve identity and asymmetry. Body T-pose: horizontal arms, straight elbows/wrists, slightly separated legs, forward knees/toes. Keep a natural shoe gap front/back; side-view projected limb overlap is normal, do not change pose to reveal limbs.

Left/right are character-relative: left face/toes point screen-left, right screen-right. Front toward viewer, back away. State asymmetrical ornaments/occlusion. No mirrored substitutes.
Match top/eyes/chin/long-hair landmarks for head; neck/shoulders/wrists/hem/soles for body. Each set coherent; head/body may use different scale.

Eight separate color images preserve original painting style/material identity. No mandatory extra balance set or separate-parts generation. Hidden areas are conservative recorded reconstruction. Depth maps are independent derived files.

## Resting hair volume, not wind volume

Before generating head color views, distinguish actual hair density/shape from wind, motion, perspective and rim-light effects in the original artwork. Reconstruct the natural gravity-resting hairstyle in still air. Do not turn the outer envelope of flying strands into extra solid hair mass, increase thickness to fill airborne gaps, or invent a larger rounded helmet. Preserve real length, density, parting, intentional curls/spikes, ornaments, painting style and facial detail. Cross-check front/back/profiles for the same plausible resting volume. This applies to both simplified and reference-faithful modes: faithful preserves authored hairstyle design, not incidental wind deformation. If the intended resting form is genuinely ambiguous, inspect other original references and isolate the uncertain feature rather than inventing bulk. Derived depth maps still follow the exact current paired color silhouette and must not reinterpret hair.
